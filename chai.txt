Chai Junction: Scaling a Tea Kiosk Chain Beyond the Metros

In 2019, two IIT Kanpur graduates, Rohan Mehta and Ananya Iyer, opened the first Chai Junction kiosk outside a coaching centre in Kota. Their idea was simple: hygienic, consistent masala chai at Rs 20 a cup, served in under 90 seconds, with a loyalty app that gave every tenth cup free. By March 2024, Chai Junction had grown to 140 kiosks across 11 tier-2 cities, with annual revenue of Rs 38 crore and an average of 410 cups sold per kiosk per day.

Most kiosks are franchise-owned. A franchisee pays a one-time fee of Rs 6 lakh and a monthly royalty of 8 percent of sales. In return, Chai Junction supplies a premixed tea blend, branded cups, and training. The company buys tea leaves directly from three estates in Assam, which has kept its tea cost about 15 percent below what local tea stalls pay. The loyalty app has 6.2 lakh registered users, and 35 percent of all orders now come through the app.

However, the founders face new pressure. In 2023, a well-funded national cafe brand launched a "chai and snacks" format priced at Rs 25 a cup, opening 60 outlets in the same cities within a year. Local tea stalls, which charge Rs 10 to Rs 12, have also started offering online ordering through food delivery apps. At the same time, milk prices rose 12 percent in 2023, and Chai Junction absorbed the increase instead of raising prices, cutting its operating margin from 14 percent to 9 percent.

Franchisee satisfaction is also slipping. A survey in January 2024 showed that 22 of the 140 franchisees were unhappy with the 8 percent royalty, saying their profits had fallen after the national brand arrived. Three franchisees closed their kiosks in the last six months. Store audits found that 30 percent of kiosks did not follow the 90-second service standard during peak hours.

Rohan wants to raise Rs 50 crore from investors to open 200 new kiosks in smaller towns, where rents are lower and competition is weaker. Ananya disagrees. She believes the company should first fix quality and franchisee economics in existing cities, perhaps by cutting the royalty to 6 percent, and only then expand. The board meeting is in four weeks, and the founders must present one clear strategy.
